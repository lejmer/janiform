\section{Purpose}

Derive the normalized terminal density used to change from the physical
terminal law to the pricing terminal law. The goal is to show that activity
premia and the scalar return tilt define a valid likelihood ratio when the
terminal cumulant is finite.

\section{Setup}

Let
\[
K_{t,T}(v,\gamma_+,\gamma_-)
=
\log\mathbb E^P\!\left[
\exp\!\left(
vX_{t,T}+\gamma_+N_{t,T}^++\gamma_-N_{t,T}^-
\right)
\mid\mathcal F_t
\right]
\]
be the physical stochastic terminal cumulant produced by the adapter in
\texttt{07\_terminal\_cumulant\_adapter.tex}. The deterministic terminal
component \(d_{t,T}\) is not included in \(K_{t,T}\).

Let \(\pi_+,\pi_->0\), and write
\[
\gamma_+=\log\pi_+,
\qquad
\gamma_-=\log\pi_-.
\]

\section{Result}

\noindent\textbf{Identity.} For every admissible real \(\theta\) and
strictly positive \(\pi_\pm\), define
\[
M_T(\theta,\pi_+,\pi_-)
=
\exp\!\left(
\theta X_{t,T}
+\log(\pi_+)N_{t,T}^+
+\log(\pi_-)N_{t,T}^-
-K_{t,T}(\theta,\log\pi_+,\log\pi_-)
\right).
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb E^P\!\left[
M_T(\theta,\pi_+,\pi_-)\mid\mathcal F_t
\right]
=1.
\]

\section{Derivation}

By definition of \(K_{t,T}\),
\[
\exp\!\left(K_{t,T}(\theta,\gamma_+,\gamma_-)\right)
=
\mathbb E^P\!\left[
\exp\!\left(
\theta X_{t,T}+\gamma_+N_{t,T}^++\gamma_-N_{t,T}^-
\right)
\mid\mathcal F_t
\right].
\]
Set \(\gamma_\pm=\log\pi_\pm\). The density is
\[
M_T
=
\frac{
\exp\!\left(
\theta X_{t,T}+\gamma_+N_{t,T}^++\gamma_-N_{t,T}^-
\right)
}{
\exp\!\left(K_{t,T}(\theta,\gamma_+,\gamma_-)\right)
}.
\]
Taking the conditional physical expectation gives
\[
\mathbb E^P[M_T\mid\mathcal F_t]
=
\frac{
\mathbb E^P\!\left[
\exp\!\left(
\theta X_{t,T}+\gamma_+N_{t,T}^++\gamma_-N_{t,T}^-
\right)
\mid\mathcal F_t
\right]
}{
\exp\!\left(K_{t,T}(\theta,\gamma_+,\gamma_-)\right)
}
=1.
\]

Since \(\theta\), \(\gamma_+\), and \(\gamma_-\) are real, and since
\(\pi_\pm>0\), the numerator is strictly positive. Therefore \(M_T\) is a
positive normalized terminal density on the admissible domain.

\section{Domain and validity}

The result is valid when \(K_{t,T}(\theta,\log\pi_+,\log\pi_-)\) is finite,
the affine schedule domain constraints hold for the corresponding adapted
query, \(\theta\) is real, and \(\pi_\pm>0\). The normalized density alone
does not impose the martingale condition. That condition determines which
\(\theta\) is used for pricing.

\section{Consequences}

\begin{itemize}
    \item The denominator cumulant is the log normalizer of the pricing density.
    \item Activity premia are public pricing-kernel coordinates, while normalization is handled by subtracting \(K_{t,T}\).
    \item The density normalization guarantees \(\phi_Q(0)=1\) once the risk-neutral characteristic function is formed.
    \item A finite normalized density is necessary but not sufficient for no-arbitrage pricing.
\end{itemize}
