# Assumptions

This inventory lists the assumptions Janiform relies on as a mathematical model and empirical modeling framework. Each assumption should support a derivation, admissibility condition, calibration argument, validation comparison, or limitation in the paper.

| ID  | Assumption                                                                                                                                                                         | Needed for                                                                                                                                       |
|-----|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| A01 | Asset prices are strictly positive and represented through finite log prices and log returns.                                                                                      | Defines the return state used by the model, observation map, and option pricing.                                                                 |
| A02 | Time is represented by ordered finite intervals with positive duration.                                                                                                            | Supports interval rates, Hawkes decay, mean-reversion scaling, affine schedule composition, and maturity construction.                           |
| A03 | Market open and closed states are known over the modeled calendar.                                                                                                                 | Separates open-market trading, open news jumps, and closed-session gap formation.                                                                |
| A04 | Endogenous buy and sell trade events occur only during open-market time.                                                                                                           | Distinguishes trade activity from closed-session information shocks.                                                                             |
| A05 | Hawkes reaction state decays over elapsed calendar time, including during closed-market periods.                                                                                   | Preserves excitation memory across gaps while preventing closed-session trades.                                                                  |
| A06 | Predictable trade seasonality is deterministic, finite, and non-negative.                                                                                                          | Provides a calendar-driven baseline for trade intensity.                                                                                         |
| A07 | Trade seasonality modulates only the immigrant trade baseline, not the Hawkes reaction state.                                                                                      | Keeps predictable activity and endogenous excitation mathematically separable.                                                                   |
| A08 | News arrivals have their own intensity law and are not modulated by trade seasonality.                                                                                             | Separates information arrival from ordinary trading seasonality.                                                                                 |
| A09 | Scheduled news times are known conditional on the information set used for the interval.                                                                                           | Makes known-news arrivals deterministic conditional on the schedule.                                                                             |
| A10 | Surprise-news arrivals are conditionally Poisson with finite non-negative open and closed baseline intensities.                                                                    | Defines the stochastic law for unscheduled information shocks.                                                                                   |
| A11 | Surprise-news calendar multipliers are deterministic and non-negative.                                                                                                             | Allows time-varying surprise-news risk without introducing a second stochastic news intensity.                                                   |
| A12 | The open and closed portions of each interval are well-defined and add up consistently.                                                                                            | Allows interval news rates and market-state routing to be aggregated.                                                                            |
| A13 | A closed-session news arrival has a well-defined first subsequent reopening time.                                                                                                  | Makes closed-session news enter observed returns as a reopening gap.                                                                             |
| A14 | News severity follows a positive gamma mark with unit mean under the chosen parameterization.                                                                                      | Identifies severity scale separately from activity and return-jump scales.                                                                       |
| A15 | Conditional on severity, news return marks are normal with finite drift and non-negative volatility scale.                                                                         | Provides the news mark MGF and jump-return law.                                                                                                  |
| A16 | Active news return jumps are coupled to activity shocks. A zero activity-coupling scale implies zero return-jump drift and volatility for that channel.                            | Prevents return-only active news events and preserves the shared-severity interpretation.                                                        |
| A17 | Open-session news marks enter open-jump returns, while closed-session news marks enter gap returns at reopening.                                                                   | Maintains the mathematical distinction between `M_t` and `G_t`.                                                                                  |
| A18 | Primitive innovations are independent before coupling through activity, long variance, news severity, and observation state.                                                       | Enables conditional factorization of the model law.                                                                                              |
| A19 | Hawkes immigrant intensity is finite and strictly positive.                                                                                                                        | Ensures a non-degenerate baseline event process.                                                                                                 |
| A20 | Hawkes decay is finite and strictly positive.                                                                                                                                      | Makes excitation decay, stationarity, and affine transform equations well-defined.                                                               |
| A21 | Hawkes self, cross, and news excitation loadings are finite and non-negative.                                                                                                      | Preserves non-negative event intensities and branching interpretation.                                                                           |
| A22 | The de-seasonalized Hawkes kernel has spectral radius below one.                                                                                                                   | Ensures finite stationary branching multipliers.                                                                                                 |
| A23 | Initial Hawkes reaction state is finite and non-negative.                                                                                                                          | Provides a valid starting state for event intensities and affine transforms.                                                                     |
| A24 | Baseline and endogenous integrated activities are finite and non-negative on each side.                                                                                            | Provides valid side activity clocks.                                                                                                             |
| A25 | Side-specific excitation-to-return-clock exposure scales are finite and strictly positive.                                                                                         | Ensures endogenous activity produces valid effective return time.                                                                                |
| A26 | Effective buy and sell return clocks are finite and non-negative.                                                                                                                  | Makes side time changes and conditional characteristic functions valid.                                                                          |
| A27 | NIG side-driver parameters are finite and remain in the NIG admissible domain.                                                                                                     | Ensures valid side cumulants, MGFs, and characteristic exponents.                                                                                |
| A28 | The sell-side skew shift is negative and keeps the sell-side NIG driver admissible.                                                                                                | Encodes directional skew without leaving the NIG family.                                                                                         |
| A29 | Buy and sell NIG drivers are conditionally independent given the clocks and state variables.                                                                                       | Supports side-factorization of the trading-return characteristic function.                                                                       |
| A30 | Side mean compensation uses each side's unit-business-time mean.                                                                                                                   | Makes the compensated trading block zero-mean conditional on activity clocks.                                                                    |
| A31 | News-jump and gap increments are finite.                                                                                                                                           | Keeps log returns and terminal transforms finite.                                                                                                |
| A32 | Long-variance mean-reversion speed is finite and strictly positive.                                                                                                                | Provides a stationary pull for `Z_t`.                                                                                                            |
| A33 | Long-variance diffusion scale is finite and non-negative.                                                                                                                          | Makes the square-root diffusion component admissible.                                                                                            |
| A34 | Long-variance event jump loadings are finite and non-negative.                                                                                                                     | Keeps event feedback as positive variance shocks.                                                                                                |
| A35 | The event-feedback-adjusted long-variance mean-reversion level is strictly positive.                                                                                               | Makes the unit-stationary-mean normalization valid.                                                                                              |
| A36 | The long-variance first-moment guard holds: expected event-feedback load is smaller than mean-reversion pull.                                                                      | Ensures a finite positive stationary mean for `Z_t`.                                                                                             |
| A37 | Initial and terminal long-variance states are finite and strictly positive.                                                                                                        | Supports baseline clock scaling and affine initialization.                                                                                       |
| A38 | Long variance changes baseline return-clock exposure but not event-arrival intensity.                                                                                              | Keeps event timing and return-amplitude memory distinct.                                                                                         |
| A39 | Baseline clock scaling uses the conditional mean of `Z_t` over open-market exposure.                                                                                               | Connects long-variance dynamics to interval-level business time.                                                                                 |
| A40 | Observed-state mean-reversion speed is finite and non-negative.                                                                                                                    | Defines temporary deviation relaxation in `D_t`.                                                                                                 |
| A41 | Observed log state decomposes as `X_t = A_t^Omega + D_t`.                                                                                                                          | Separates market-pressure anchor-pressure movement from temporary deviation.                                                                     |
| A42 | Market pressure state `Omega_t` has finite ordered side-balance support, finite physical CTMC transition rates, and finite anchor drift loading per signed-balance exposure.       | Makes the universal market pressure state well-defined without future-event replay.                                                              |
| A43 | Market pressure changes persistent baseline side composition while preserving total event-arrival activity.                                                                         | Separates long-lived signed imbalance from total activity and short-lived Hawkes excitation.                                                     |
| A44 | Market-pressure anchor-pressure drift has finite scale and finite signed-balance exposure routing.                                                                                  | Defines `dA_t^Omega = gamma_Omega b(Omega_t) dE_t^Omega` as anchor movement induced by signed imbalance exposure.                                |
| A45 | If an observed tick grid is used, tick size is finite and strictly positive.                                                                                                       | Defines a valid discrete observation map.                                                                                                        |
| A46 | Tick-grid projection maps latent log price to observed price while carrying residual displacement forward.                                                                         | Preserves consistency between latent and observed paths.                                                                                         |
| A47 | The affine transform uses the same Hawkes, news-severity, and long-variance parameters as the physical model.                                                                      | Ties transform-based moments and pricing to the same structural law.                                                                             |
| A48 | Affine schedule intervals have finite non-negative news intensities and finite deterministic factors.                                                                              | Makes schedule composition and news transforms finite.                                                                                           |
| A49 | Affine loadings remain inside the admissible moment domain.                                                                                                                        | Ensures finite cumulants and valid transform queries.                                                                                            |
| A50 | The affine ODE/Riccati solution exists and remains finite over the pricing or moment horizon.                                                                                      | Makes the exponential-affine transform usable across maturities.                                                                                 |
| A51 | Terminal cumulants preserve the distinction between baseline activity, endogenous activity, unweighted market-pressure anchor movement, and mean-reversion-weighted core shocks.   | Allows pricing and sufficient-statistics queries to distinguish predictable activity, excited activity, anchor pressure, and core return shocks. |
| A52 | Activity-risk premia are finite and strictly positive.                                                                                                                             | Defines an admissible terminal pricing density.                                                                                                  |
| A53 | The martingale tilt has a non-empty admissible domain and a finite solution for the valuation setup.                                                                               | Enables construction of a risk-neutral characteristic function.                                                                                  |
| A54 | Risk-free rates and dividend yields are finite deterministic inputs over each maturity.                                                                                            | Supports discounting, forwards, terminal measure normalization, and put-call parity.                                                             |
| A55 | Terminal-affine spectral/COS pricing uses a finite deviation domain and enough modes for the payoff projection being evaluated.                                                    | Makes option values interpretable as numerical projections of the terminal characteristic function.                                              |
| A56 | Payoffs are European vanilla calls and puts in v1.                                                                                                                                 | Defines the payoff class covered by the pricing propositions and parity relation.                                                                |
| A57 | Calibration data has aligned timestamps, prices, event labels, market-state labels, and option observations.                                                                       | Makes staged calibration and joint option refinement meaningful.                                                                                 |
| A58 | Distinct model layers are sufficiently identifiable from the selected evidence windows and constraints.                                                                            | Justifies staged calibration while exposing identifiability risk.                                                                                |
| A59 | Empirical and simulated comparisons use the same calendar, horizons, market-state rules, and aggregation definitions.                                                              | Makes validation and ablation evidence comparable.                                                                                               |
| A60 | Stochastic evidence is conditional on specified random seeds or equivalent replayable random streams.                                                                              | Makes simulated evidence reproducible.                                                                                                           |
| A61 | Empirical figures and ablations are conditional on specified data transformations, model inputs, and sampling policies.                                                            | Makes paper evidence scientifically recoverable.                                                                                                 |
| A62 | Numerical tolerances, truncation choices, solver limits, and convergence gates are explicit model or numerical policies.                                                           | Prevents hidden heuristics from becoming unstated assumptions.                                                                                   |
| A63 | Inputs used in model equations are finite and satisfy their admissibility domains before the equations are evaluated.                                                              | Keeps derivations and numerical procedures inside their stated domains.                                                                          |
| A64 | Seasonal profiles are stable enough over the fitted and simulated horizon to be represented as deterministic functions.                                                            | Justifies deterministic time-of-day, day-of-week, day-of-year, and special-day profiles.                                                         |
| A65 | Buy and sell event signs contain economically meaningful information about future activity, return asymmetry, and volatility.                                                      | Justifies directional clocks and asymmetric excitation.                                                                                          |
| A66 | A low-dimensional Hawkes reaction state captures the endogenous activity clustering relevant to Janiform's target outputs.                                                         | Explains why full limit-order-book state is not a required state variable.                                                                       |
| A67 | Endogenous excitation and news activity shocks are adequately represented by additive intensity effects over the modeled regime.                                                   | States the linear-excitation belief and identifies saturation or nonlinear queue effects as limitations.                                         |
| A68 | News impact is adequately summarized by arrival time, component type, market state, and a shared severity mark.                                                                    | Justifies the low-dimensional news layer.                                                                                                        |
| A69 | Closed-market information is adequately represented either by reopening gaps or by defining pre-market and post-market sessions as open windows with lower deterministic activity. | States the gap-model belief while allowing extended-session calendars.                                                                           |
| A70 | Persistent volatility memory is adequately represented by a finite-dimensional Markov long-variance state.                                                                         | Positions `Z_t` against rough or infinite-memory volatility alternatives.                                                                        |
| A71 | NIG side drivers combined with stochastic business time are rich enough to capture the target conditional return tails and skew.                                                   | Justifies using NIG side laws rather than an unrestricted return innovation law.                                                                 |
| A72 | Terminal risk adjustment is adequately low-dimensional in activity-risk premia, signed-imbalance exposure risk price, and the derived martingale tilt.                             | Justifies the terminal pricing measure parameterization.                                                                                         |
| A73 | Model layers leave enough separable empirical signal for staged calibration to be scientifically meaningful.                                                                       | States the empirical identifiability belief behind the calibration strategy.                                                                     |
